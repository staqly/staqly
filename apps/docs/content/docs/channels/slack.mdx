title: Slack
description: Publish to Slack channels through a Slack app, with text, files and replies in the message's thread. What to register at api.slack.com.

Staqly publishes to **Slack** through a Slack app installed in a workspace: the person signs in
with OAuth v2 and the **workspace** becomes a staqly channel. Connect as many workspaces as you like;
each post picks the Slack channel it goes to, and goes out as the app or as the person who connected.
Messages go out with `chat.postMessage`, files with `files.getUploadURLExternal` +
`files.completeUploadExternal` (the text becomes the files' message). A published target links to
the message.

## What you need

A **Slack app**, registered once per server. Its keys go into the server's environment,
the same way locally and in the cloud; a workspace can also bring its own app (see
[Developer apps](/channels/developer-apps)).

<Steps>
<Step>

### Create the app

At [api.slack.com/apps](https://api.slack.com/apps), **Create New App → From scratch**, name it
(the name shows on every message) and pick a development workspace. Then:

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| **OAuth & Permissions → Redirect URLs** | your dashboard's `/channels/callback/slack`, for example `https://staqly.example.com/channels/callback/slack`. Slack takes `https://` addresses only, so locally use a tunnel to `localhost:8080` |
| **OAuth & Permissions → Bot Token Scopes** | nothing needed: staqly asks for its scopes when someone connects (listed below) |
| **Manage Distribution** | **Activate Public Distribution** so workspaces other than the development one can install the app (no Slack Marketplace review is needed for that) |

Copy the **Client ID** and **Client Secret** from **Basic Information → App Credentials**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Give staqly the keys

Put them in the server's environment and restart it:

```bash title=".env"
PROVIDER_SLACK_CLIENT_ID=your-client-id
PROVIDER_SLACK_CLIENT_SECRET=your-client-secret
```

- **Docker:** `docker run ... --env-file .env ghcr.io/staqly/staqly:latest`, or `-e PROVIDER_SLACK_CLIENT_ID=...`.
- **Binary:** export both in your shell, then run `staqly serve` or `staqly service install` (the
  service keeps `PROVIDER_*` settings).

Until both are set, Slack shows as **soon** in **Channels → Add channel**.
`PROVIDER_SLACK_ENABLED=false` hides it altogether.

</Step>
<Step>

### Connect a workspace

**Connect with Slack** opens slack.com, where the person picks the workspace and allows the app.
The workspace shows as its address (`acme.slack.com`); connecting it again reconnects it. For
another workspace, connect again and pick that one on slack.com. Staqly asks for these bot scopes:

| Scope | Why |
|---|---|
| `chat:write` | Post messages as the app |
| `channels:read`, `groups:read` | List the public channels, and the private ones the app was invited to, to pick from |
| `channels:join` | Join a public channel a post goes to, so it can post and share files there |
| `files:write` | Upload files |
| `files:read` | Find the message an upload made, to link to it and reply under it |
| `team:read` | Show the workspace's icon (without one, its first letter, as Slack does) |
| `reactions:read` | Count the reactions under a post and the replies in its thread (**Statistics**) |
| `users:read` | Name who reacted |

And these user scopes, so a post can go out as the person who connects (**Post as**):

| Scope | Why |
|---|---|
| `chat:write` | Post messages as that person |
| `files:write`, `files:read` | Upload files as that person and find their message |
| `users.profile:read` | Show that person's photo next to **As myself** |


</Step>
</Steps>

## Limits

| | |
|---|---|
| Text | 40,000 characters (Slack's limit for a message) |
| Media | up to **10** files per message, shared together with the text |

Slack's own formatting (`*bold*`, `_italic_`, links) works in the text; `&`, `<` and `>` are sent
as written.

## Settings per post

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Show link previews** (`link_preview`) | On by default: Slack unfurls links into previews |
| **Post as** (`post_as`) | `app` (default): the app's name and icon. `me`: the person who connected Slack, with their name and photo; they must be in the channel. A channel connected before this setting existed needs reconnecting once |
| **Slack channels** (`channel`, required) | Up to 10 channels of the workspace, ids or `#names`, comma-separated: one message in each, its comments in each message's thread. The dashboard picks **#random** (else **#general**) for a new post; pick others from the list (`GET /v1/channels/{id}/options/channel`, MCP `list_channel_options`): every public channel, and the private ones after `/invite @YourApp` in them. The app joins a public channel before posting. When one channel refuses after others took the post, the target fails naming where it went, and is not retried (it would post twice) |

## Comments

A post's [thread](/concepts#threads-comments-under-a-post) goes out as replies in the message's
Slack thread, one after another with their delays.

When a post has files, Slack answers with the files, not the message; staqly asks Slack where they
were shared for a few seconds. When Slack does not say in time, the target links to the channel and
its comments are skipped; the post is out.

## Statistics

A published post's **Statistics** (`GET /v1/posts/{id}/metrics`) show, summed over every channel it went to: its
**Reactions** (each emoji with its count and who reacted) and the **Replies** in its threads. Slack has no views or
reach for a message. A workspace connected before these numbers needs reconnecting once.

## Tokens

Slack bot tokens last until the app is removed from the workspace. If the app turns on **token
rotation**, staqly renews them every 12 hours with the refresh token. Checking the channel's
connection asks Slack whether the token still works.

## When publishing fails

| Slack says | Staqly does |
|---|---|
| The token is revoked or the app was removed from the workspace | Marks the channel **Needs reconnect** and fails the target: reconnect from the row's menu |
| Slack refuses the post's channel (archived, private without the app, or the person is not in it) | Fails the target only: the workspace stays connected. Invite the app, join the channel yourself, or pick another one |
| Rate limit (429) or Slack unavailable | Retries through the job queue (after 1, 5, then 15 minutes) |
| A refused message: too long, posting restricted in the channel, files not allowed | Fails the target with Slack's reason; retrying would not help, so it does not |
| The answer never came back (a dropped connection) | Fails the target saying the message **may have been published**: Slack has no idempotency key, and sending again could post twice. Check the channel before you retry |
