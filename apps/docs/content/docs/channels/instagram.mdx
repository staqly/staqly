Staqly publishes to an **Instagram professional account** (Business or Creator) through the
Instagram API with Instagram Login: the person signs in on instagram.com, no Facebook Page is
needed. A published target links to the post on instagram.com. Accounts linked to a Facebook Page
can also connect as [Instagram Business](/channels/instagram_business), through Facebook Login.

## What you need

- A **Meta app** with the Instagram product, registered once per server. Its keys go into the
  server's environment, the same way locally and in the cloud (see
  [Developer apps](/channels/developer-apps)).
- **Media storage Instagram can reach.** Instagram does not take uploads: it downloads every picture
  and video from a link. Staqly hands it a time-limited link to the file in
  [S3-compatible storage](/self-hosting/storage), so a server that keeps media on its own disk
  can't publish to Instagram (the target fails and says why). Locally, the bucket needs an address
  on the internet, for example a tunnel to it set as `S3_PUBLIC_URL`.
- An Instagram account set to **Business** or **Creator** (Instagram app → Settings → Account type).
  Personal accounts can't publish through the API.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Create the app

At [Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/apps), click **Create app** and choose a
**Business** app. Add the **Instagram** product, then open **API setup with Instagram login**:

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| **Set up Instagram business login → Business login settings → OAuth redirect URIs** | your dashboard's `/channels/callback/instagram`, for example `https://staqly.example.com/channels/callback/instagram`. Meta takes `https://` addresses only, so locally use a tunnel to `localhost:8080` |
| **App roles → Roles → Instagram testers** | the Instagram accounts that may connect while the app is in development; each accepts the invitation in the Instagram app (Settings → Website permissions → Apps and websites → Tester invites) |

Copy the **Instagram app ID** and **Instagram app secret** from the same page (not the Meta app's
own ID).

</Step>
<Step>

### Give staqly the keys

Put them in the server's environment and restart it:

```bash title=".env"
PROVIDER_INSTAGRAM_CLIENT_ID=your-instagram-app-id
PROVIDER_INSTAGRAM_CLIENT_SECRET=your-instagram-app-secret
```

- **Docker:** `docker run ... --env-file .env ghcr.io/staqly/staqly:latest`, or `-e PROVIDER_INSTAGRAM_CLIENT_ID=...`.
- **Binary:** export both in your shell, then run `staqly serve` or `staqly service install` (the
  service keeps `PROVIDER_*` settings).

Until both are set, Instagram shows as **soon** in **Channels → Add channel**.
`PROVIDER_INSTAGRAM_ENABLED=false` hides it altogether.

</Step>
<Step>

### Connect the account

**Connect with Instagram** opens instagram.com, where the person signs in and allows the app.
Staqly asks for these scopes:

| Scope | Why |
|---|---|
| `instagram_business_basic` | Read the account's username, name and picture |
| `instagram_business_content_publish` | Publish posts, carousels, reels and stories |
| `instagram_business_manage_comments` | Post the comments of a post's thread |

The channel shows the account's name, `@username` and picture.

If Instagram signs the account in but the connection then fails with "won't let the app use it", the
account is personal, or (while the app is in testing) not one of its testers: check which account
you are signed in to on instagram.com.

</Step>
<Step>

### Before other people connect

While the app is in development, only its testers can connect. Before your customers connect their
accounts, Meta reviews the app: **App Review** for each of the three scopes (a screen recording of
staqly using it) and **Business Verification** of your company.

</Step>
</Steps>

## The 60-day sign-in

Instagram's sign-in lasts **60 days** and can be renewed. Staqly renews it on its own a day before
it runs out, so the channel stays connected as long as the account allows the app. If the person
removes the app or changes their password, the channel turns **Needs reconnect**.

## Limits

| | |
|---|---|
| Text | 2,200 characters; up to 30 hashtags |
| Media | at least one file: up to **10** pictures and videos (a carousel), **one video** for a reel, **one file** for a story |
| Pictures | **JPEG only**, up to 8 MB; feed pictures 4:5 to 1.91:1 (the composer flags a picture that doesn't fit) |
| Posts | Instagram takes up to **100 posts a day** per account through its API |

## Settings per post

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Post as** (`post_type`) | `post` (default; several files make a carousel, a single video goes out as a reel), `reel` or `story` (stories take no caption) |
| **Also show the reel in the feed** (`share_to_feed`) | Reels only; on by default |
| **Collaborators** (`collaborators`) | Feed posts: up to 3 usernames invited as collaborators; the post shows on their profiles once they accept |

Over the API: `{"post_type": "reel", "share_to_feed": "false"}` in the target's `settings`.

## Comments

A post's [thread](/concepts#threads-comments-under-a-post) goes out as comments under the post, in
order and after their delays: for example a first comment with hashtags. Comments take text only;
stories have none.

## When publishing fails

| Instagram says | Staqly does |
|---|---|
| The sign-in expired or was revoked | Marks the channel **Needs reconnect** and fails the target; reconnect it from the row's menu |
| Rate limit, Instagram unavailable, a media download that timed out | Retries through the job queue (after 1, 5, then 15 minutes) |
| A refused picture (format, size, shape), the daily limit, a restricted account | Fails the target with Instagram's reason; retrying would not help, so it does not |

Instagram has no way to ask "did this post go out?", and a new attempt would make a new post. So
when staqly sent the post but its answer never came, the target fails with "may have published"
instead of trying again: check the profile before you post it again.
