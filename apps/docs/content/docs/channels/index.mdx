A **channel** is an account staqly publishes to: an [X](/channels/x) account, a
[Facebook Page](/channels/facebook), a [Telegram](/channels/telegram) channel, a
[WordPress](/channels/wordpress) blog, or **your own automation** when staqly should hand the post
to a script or n8n workflow instead. Connect a channel once; from then on every post can target it, and a
collection's schedules can publish to it.

## Connect a channel

<Steps>
<Step>

### Pick the platform

Open **Channels** in the sidebar and click **Add channel**. The drawer lists every platform
staqly knows ([the catalog below](#the-catalog)) in groups: your own automation, social
networks, video and streaming, communities and chat, blogs and newsletters, business and
portfolio. Platforms that are not ready yet carry a **Soon** badge and cannot be connected: not built
yet, or an OAuth platform whose app keys the server doesn't have. A platform turned off on the
server is not listed (see [Platforms on a server](#platforms-on-a-server)).

Someone without access to Staqly (a client) can connect their account too: send them a
[connect link](/channels/connect-links).

</Step>
<Step>

### Connect it

How you connect depends on the platform's `auth`:

| `auth` | What happens |
|---|---|
| `token` | The form asks for the platform's credentials (for example a bot token and chat, or a handle and app password). Staqly checks them against the platform, reads the account's name and picture, and stores them sealed. |
| `oauth2` | **Connect with ...** sends your browser to the platform; after you allow access it comes back to `/channels/callback/<provider>` and the channel is created. The platform needs a [developer app](/channels/developer-apps) whose keys are in the server's environment; without them it is listed as **Soon**. |

### Pick the accounts

Some sign-ins can publish as several accounts: the Facebook Pages you manage, the Instagram accounts
linked to them, LinkedIn Pages, YouTube channels, Google Business Profile locations, Tumblr blogs,
MeWe groups. When there is only one, staqly connects it right away. When there are
several, the platform sends you back to a list: tick the ones you want and click **Connect**. Every
account you pick becomes its own channel (one already connected is reconnected instead), and nothing is
ticked for you. The list waits ten minutes; after that, connect again. The same list appears on a
[connect link](/channels/connect-links) page, where every picked account takes one of the link's uses.

Over the API the platform's answer lands on the dashboard's callback page with `?pending=<id>`;
`GET /v1/pending-connections/{id}` lists the accounts and `POST /v1/pending-connections/{id}` with
`{"account_ids": [...]}` connects them (`channels:manage`).
| `none` | **Your own automation**: nothing to connect. Staqly asks your script or workflow to publish and waits for its result. See [Your own automation](/channels/your-own-automation). |

Give the channel a **Name** (it defaults to the account's name) and, where the provider offers
them, default **settings** for new targets (who can reply on X, say).

</Step>
<Step>

### Use it

Pick the channel's avatar in the composer, or the channel in a schedule. The composer shows the
platform's character limit per channel, gives each picked channel a tab for a text of its own
and a folded **Settings** card under the editor with that platform's options (who can reply, a
community, labels, a last comment, accounts that repost it...). Each platform's page lists its settings.

</Step>
</Steps>

From a terminal or a script, your own automation and token platforms (with their `fields`)
connect with one request; OAuth platforms such as X only through the browser:

```bash tab="curl"
API=http://localhost:3333

# what each platform needs
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $STAQLY_API_KEY" $API/v1/providers | jq '.data[] | {id, auth, status, credential_fields}'

# your own automation: staqly announces each post to your agents and webhooks
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $STAQLY_API_KEY" -X POST $API/v1/channels -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"provider":"custom","name":"My n8n publisher"}'

# your own automation that staqly calls itself (see Your own automation)
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $STAQLY_API_KEY" -X POST $API/v1/channels -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"provider":"custom","name":"My endpoint","fields":{"url":"https://n8n.example.com/webhook/staqly"}}'
```

```bash tab="CLI"
staqly channels providers
staqly channels connect custom --name "My n8n publisher"
staqly channels connect custom --name "My endpoint" --field url=https://n8n.example.com/webhook/staqly
```

```text tab="MCP"
list_providers
connect_channel { "provider": "custom", "name": "My n8n publisher" }
```

Creating, changing and removing channels needs the `channels:manage` scope in cloud mode;
listing them needs `channels:read`. Credentials are never returned by any call.

### Platforms on a server

Every platform has a switch in the server's environment, locally and in the cloud:

```bash title=".env"
PROVIDER_X_ENABLED=true            # false: turned off on this server
PROVIDER_X_CLIENT_ID=...           # OAuth platforms: the developer app
PROVIDER_X_CLIENT_SECRET=...
```

- `PROVIDER_<ID>_ENABLED=false` hides the platform from the catalog and the dashboard and refuses
  connecting it and new posts on its channels (`409 provider_disabled`). Posts already scheduled
  on its channels still publish, and can still be edited.
- Enabled but without `_CLIENT_ID` and `_CLIENT_SECRET`, an OAuth platform is listed as **soon**.
- Restart the server after changing them. `.env.example` has a block for every platform.

### Platforms that need https

Some platforms send the browser back to staqly only at a secure (`https://`) address after you
allow the app: Facebook, Instagram Business, Threads, TikTok and Slack (`needs_https` in
`GET /v1/providers`). On a dashboard opened at a plain `http://` address, such as
`http://localhost:8080`, they show **Not available** in **Add channel** and can't be connected.

To connect them on your own computer:

1. Give staqly an https address that reaches the dashboard on port 8080: a tunnel (Cloudflare
   Tunnel, Tailscale Funnel, ngrok) or a reverse proxy with a certificate, for example
   `https://staqly.example.com`.
2. Tell staqly that address and restart it: `PUBLIC_DASHBOARD_URL=https://staqly.example.com`
   (Docker: `-e PUBLIC_DASHBOARD_URL=...`). Platforms send people back to
   `<that address>/channels/callback/<platform>`, which is what you register as the redirect URL
   of the platform's app.
3. Open the dashboard **at that address**, sign in there and connect the platform. A connection
   started at `http://localhost:8080` can't finish at the https address: the browser keeps their
   sign-ins apart (**The connection did not start in this browser**).

Once connected, the channel publishes whichever address you use the dashboard at, localhost
included; only connecting (and reconnecting) needs the https address. **Settings → System →
Platforms** checks the setup for each platform.

All limits in one place: [Limits](/limits).

"Media" means images and videos unless noted. The limits are the platforms' published ones and
drive the composer's counters and `GET /v1/posts/{id}/preview`. Each available platform has its own
page here, with what to register and which settings it takes.

## Channel status and health

| Status | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Connected** | Ready to publish. | — |
| **Needs reconnect** | A publish, a test or a token refresh failed with the stored credentials; the row shows the error. Targets on it fail until it is reconnected. | **Reconnect** in the row's menu: OAuth platforms go through the platform again (the same account reconnects the existing channel), token platforms take fresh credentials (`PATCH /v1/channels/{id}` with `fields`). |
| **Disabled** | Turned off by you. New posts cannot target it and schedules skip it. | **Enable** in the menu (`PATCH` with `status: connected`). |

**Test connection** (`POST /v1/channels/{id}/test`, MCP `test_channel`, CLI
`staqly channels test`) checks the stored credentials against the platform now; a failing test
marks the channel *Needs reconnect*, a passing one clears it. It is rate-limited (burst 5, then
10 per minute). OAuth sign-ins are refreshed by a `refresh_channel` [job](/concepts#jobs) before
they expire. The `channel.needs_reconnect` event is one of the three an
[alert](/automation/alerts) sends by default.

## Managing channels

The **Channels** page lists every channel with its picture, name, handle, platform, status and
activity (last post, next post, posts in the last 30 days). Each row's menu has **Settings**,
**Test connection**, **Edit connection** (platforms connected with fields, such as your own
automation), **Move to group** (once there is more than one group), **Disable** or **Enable**,
**Reconnect** (when it needs one) and **Remove**. **Settings** renames the channel, picks its **group** and
sets **Links in posts**, what the channel does with the links in a post's text when it publishes:

| Links in posts | `https://example.com/a` goes out as |
|---|---|
| **Keep links** | as written (the default on most platforms) |
| **Remove links** | nothing: the link is taken out |
| **Show links as plain text** | `example[.]com/a`, readable but not a link |

Platforms that charge more for links or show posts with them to fewer people offer only the last
two: [X](/channels/x) removes them unless you choose plain text. The post keeps its text; the
character limit counts what the channel receives, the composer says in red which channels change
which links, and `GET /v1/posts/{id}/preview` lists them per target (`links_found`, plus a
warning). Over the API it is the channel's `settings.links` (the channel's `links` field shows
the mode in use; the catalog's `limits.links` and `limits.link_modes` give each platform's
default and choices).

### Channel groups

Every channel belongs to exactly one **group**, for example one group per client. A workspace
always has a **Default** group, which can't be deleted; new channels land there unless you pick
another. With more than one group, the Channels page lists the channels group by group (each one
folds open and closed), the composer's channel picker shows them under their group's name, and a
group filter keeps one group. **New group** creates one, a group's menu renames it, and deleting a
group moves its channels to Default.

Over the API: `GET /v1/channel-groups` lists the groups (Default first) with how many channels each
holds; `POST /v1/channel-groups` creates one (`name`, unique in the workspace; optional `color`
`#rrggbb` and `position`), `PATCH /v1/channel-groups/{id}` renames it, and
`DELETE /v1/channel-groups/{id}` deletes it (`409 default_group` for Default). A channel's
`group_id` says where it is; `POST /v1/channels` takes a `group_id`, `PATCH /v1/channels/{id}` with
`group_id` moves it, and `GET /v1/channels?group=grp_...` keeps one group.

`GET /v1/channels` and `GET /v1/channels/{id}` also return each channel's `activity`, read from
its targets: `last_published_at` and `last_post_id` (the last post that went out on it),
`next_publish_at` (its next scheduled post) and `published_30d`. Webhook payloads carry the
channel without it.

Removing a channel removes the targets on it from every post; posts with no other
target become drafts.

## Security

- Credentials and developer app secrets are sealed with AES-256-GCM. The key is generated into
  `DATA_DIR/secret.key` on first use, or comes from `STAQLY_SECRET_KEY` (32 bytes, base64).
  **Back it up with the database**: without it every channel has to be reconnected.
- No response ever carries a credential or a client secret, not even to an admin.
- The OAuth `state` is signed and bound to the browser that started the flow with a short-lived
  cookie, so a callback cannot be replayed into another session.
- Publishing calls the platform from the server; your browser never holds a platform token.
