---
title: Billing with Stripe
description: Let cloud workspaces pay for Starter, Plus, Pro or Business by card, monthly or yearly, with Stripe Checkout and the customer portal.
---

In cloud mode, Staqly can sell its [plans](/plans) through Stripe. The account owner picks a plan
and a billing period (monthly, or yearly at 20 % off or more), pays on Stripe's checkout page, and the
workspace moves to that plan as soon as Stripe confirms the payment. Without Stripe settings,
billing stays off and you change plans with [`set-plan`](/self-hosting/operator-cli).

With billing on, a new cloud workspace has **no plan** (read-only) until its owner starts a
subscription: Starter's checkout starts **Stripe's 7-day trial with a card required**
(`subscription_data[trial_period_days]=7`, `payment_method_collection=always`, cancelled if no card
is on file when it ends), once per workspace and to one workspace of an account at a time (a workspace
added while another of the account is trialing is charged at once; `GET /v1/billing` → `trial`); Plus,
Pro and Business are charged at once. Switching to another workspace and adding one stay possible from a
read-only workspace. Without
billing (a cloud server without Stripe), a new workspace gets a 7-day trial of `TRIAL_PLAN`
(Starter by default) with no card, run by the server; test servers set `TRIAL_PLAN=pro`. When a plan
ends unpaid the workspace becomes read-only until a plan is paid for (or set by the operator). `GET /v1/setup` reports `billing_enabled`, and the dashboard words its pricing by it.

Local installs have no plans and no billing.

## Set it up

<Steps>
<Step>

### Create the prices with your own Stripe account

In your Stripe account (test mode first), create one product per plan and one recurring price per
plan and billing period: eight prices in all. Prices are **per seat** (Stripe's "per unit"
pricing, with the per-user amount below): Staqly sets each subscription's quantity to the
workspace's members. Give each price a **lookup key**: Staqly finds the
price by it, so no price ids go into its settings.

| Plan | Monthly price per user, lookup key | Yearly price per user, lookup key |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | €15 / month, `staqly_starter_month` | €144 / year, `staqly_starter_year` |
| Plus | €29 / month, `staqly_plus_month` | €276 / year, `staqly_plus_year` |
| Pro | €59 / month, `staqly_pro_month` | €564 / year, `staqly_pro_year` |
| Business | €99 / month, `staqly_business_month` | €948 / year, `staqly_business_year` |

Also set the metadata `staqly_plan` (`starter`, `plus`, `pro` or `business`) and `staqly_unit`
(`seat`) on each price, so a subscription changed in Stripe itself still maps back to its plan. The lookup key works for that
too. Don't add a trial to the prices: Starter's checkout asks for it. Prices are in **euros** and
**tax exclusive** (`tax_behavior=exclusive`): with Stripe Tax on, Checkout collects the billing
address and tax ID and adds VAT (`automatic_tax[enabled]=true`). A price's tax behaviour can't
change; a new one replaces it like an amount.

**Extra posts to X** are one-time prices with the lookup keys `staqly_credits_x_200`,
`staqly_credits_x_1000` and `staqly_credits_x_4000` (€10, €40, €150; metadata `staqly_kind=credits`,
`staqly_provider=x`, `staqly_posts`). `POST /v1/billing/credits/checkout` opens a one-time Checkout;
the webhook's `checkout.session.completed` (mode `payment`) adds the posts once per session, expiring
at the end of the billing period.

Prices can't change their amount in Stripe. To change one, create a new price, move the lookup key
to it (Stripe's "transfer lookup key") and archive the old price; existing subscriptions keep the
old price until they switch plans. Staqly caches a looked-up price for 10 minutes.

**Seats.** The checkout starts the subscription with one seat per member. When someone accepts an
invitation, leaves or is removed, Staqly queues a job that sets the subscription item's quantity to
the member count, prorated from the moment the membership changed (`proration_date`):

- A person added mid-period is charged at once for the rest of the period
  (`proration_behavior=always_invoice`). Waiting for the next invoice would leave that charge
  unpaid when a subscription is cancelled at the period's end.
- A person removed mid-period is credited for the unused rest on the next invoice
  (`create_prorations`).
- Added and removed in the same period, the charge and the credit net to the days they were a
  member. Changes made seconds apart, before the job runs, are coalesced: the job sets the
  absolute member count and prorates from the latest change.
- During the Starter trial nothing is prorated; the first invoice uses the seats at the trial's
  end. Yearly plans prorate the same way over the year.

A webhook reporting another quantity (changed in Stripe) queues the same job, which puts it back,
so turn off "customers can update quantities" in the Stripe customer portal settings. Open
invitations are not billed.

**Prices shown.** With billing on, the plan list (the dashboard's plan picker, the Billing page,
`GET /v1/plans` and MCP) shows the amounts Stripe charges, read from the prices by lookup key and
cached for 10 minutes. A price Stripe doesn't have, an amount that isn't whole dollars, or a Stripe
that can't be reached falls back to the built-in amounts.

A `STRIPE_PRICE_*` value that isn't a price id (such as the `price_...` placeholder) is ignored, and a
pinned price Stripe doesn't have is retried once with the lookup key; leave them unset normally.

Instead of lookup keys, you can give the price ids:
`STRIPE_PRICE_<STARTER|PLUS|PRO|BUSINESS>_<MONTHLY|YEARLY>=price_...` (a set id wins over the
lookup key).

</Step>
<Step>

### Add the webhook endpoint

Under **Developers → Webhooks**, add an endpoint:

- URL: `https://staqly.example.com/v1/billing/webhook` (your `PUBLIC_API_URL`)
- Events: `checkout.session.completed`, `customer.subscription.created`,
  `customer.subscription.updated`, `customer.subscription.deleted`,
  `entitlements.active_entitlement_summary.updated` (add-ons and per-customer features)

Copy its signing secret (`whsec_` followed by random characters).

</Step>
<Step>

### Configure staqly

```bash title=".env"
STRIPE_SECRET_KEY=sk_test_51ExampleKey
STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET=whsec_from_the_stripe_webhook_endpoint
PUBLIC_DASHBOARD_URL=https://staqly.example.com   # Stripe sends people back here (make cloud sets it from STAQLY_DOMAIN)
```

Billing is on only in cloud mode (`STAQLY_MODE=cloud`) and only when both `STRIPE_` variables are
set. A plan and period whose price isn't found answers `409 not_available` at checkout. Restart
Staqly (`make cloud` again recreates the container with the new `.env`).

After checkout or the portal, Stripe sends people back to the **Billing** page:
`PUBLIC_DASHBOARD_URL` (else `PUBLIC_API_URL`) + `/settings/billing`, with `?checkout=success` or
`?checkout=canceled` after checkout and `?checkout=switched` after a plan switch.

</Step>
<Step>

### Turn on the customer portal

In Stripe, under **Settings → Billing → Customer portal**, allow customers to update payment
methods, see invoices and cancel. **Manage subscription** in the dashboard opens this portal.
Plan switches go through Staqly's plan picker, so the portal doesn't need to offer them (if it
does, list the eight prices there; Staqly maps them back by lookup key or metadata).

</Step>
</Steps>

### Test locally

The development stack (`make dev`, see
[Docker Compose](/self-hosting/docker-compose#development-stack)) has an opt-in Stripe CLI
container that forwards test-mode events to the API. Put this in `.env` in the repository root:

```bash title=".env"
STAQLY_MODE=cloud
STRIPE_SECRET_KEY=sk_test_51ExampleKey
COMPOSE_PROFILES=stripe
```

Then print the signing secret of the Stripe CLI, add it as `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` and start the
stack:

```bash
make stripe-secret                  # prints whsec_..., put it in .env as STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET
make dev
```

Without Docker, run the Stripe CLI yourself and use the secret it prints:

```bash
stripe listen --forward-to localhost:3333/v1/billing/webhook
```

## How plans follow the subscription

| Stripe subscription status | Workspace plan |
|---|---|
| `active`, `trialing` | The plan of the subscription's price until the end of the paid period plus 3 days of grace |
| `past_due` (a renewal failed) | Unchanged until the previously paid end plus grace; then read-only |
| `canceled`, `unpaid`, `incomplete_expired`, `paused` | The plan ends now: the workspace is read-only until it pays again |
| `incomplete` (first payment pending) | Unchanged |

- Staqly reads `checkout.session.completed` and `customer.subscription.created`, `.updated`,
  `.deleted`, `.paused` and `.resumed`. Other event types are acknowledged and ignored.
- Each Stripe event is applied once, even when Stripe retries it.
- When a workspace replaced its subscription, the old one ending doesn't touch the new one.
- Every change is recorded in the activity log as `workspace.plan_changed` with
  `reason: "stripe:<status>"`.
- A suspended workspace can still open Checkout or the portal to pay.
- A workspace with a live subscription that picks another plan or period is switched
  (`proration_behavior=create_prorations`): Stripe charges or credits the difference.
- Deleting a workspace cancels its subscription right away.

## Add-ons and one customer's features (Stripe Entitlements)

The plans in the server are the baseline. On top of them, a workspace gets whatever its Stripe subscription
grants through [Stripe Entitlements](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/entitlements): each product on the
subscription (the plan, an add-on, a deal) carries features, and a feature's metadata says what it does:

| Feature metadata | Effect |
|---|---|
| `staqly_limit_<limit>` = `+10` | Adds to a limit: `channels`, `alerts`, `webhooks`, `api_keys`, `posts_per_month`, `x_posts_per_period`, `storage_gb`, `max_upload_mb` |
| `staqly_limit_<limit>` = `unlimited` | Lifts the limit |
| `staqly_enable` = `data_export` | Turns backups and full exports on |

- Deltas from several features add up; a limit a plan has unlimited stays unlimited; unknown keys are ignored.
- The server stores the active features on the workspace and applies them in every limit check. It reads
  them from Stripe after `entitlements.active_entitlement_summary.updated` and after every subscription event,
  in a background job, so a request never waits on Stripe.
- Without billing (no Stripe keys) nothing grants features and the plans apply as they are.
- The workspace's plan (<Method m="GET" path="/v1/workspace/usage" /> and the Billing page) shows the effective
  limits, the plan's own (`base_limits`) and what each feature added (`limit_bonuses`), e.g. "Channels 25
  (15 + 10 from Channels pack)".

**Add-ons.** A product with metadata `staqly_kind=addon` and `staqly_addon=<id>`, whose prices carry the lookup
keys `staqly_addon_<id>_month` and `staqly_addon_<id>_year` (per workspace, quantity 1), is offered on the
Billing page. Adding one puts it on the live subscription as an item of its own in the subscription's
interval, charged at once for the rest of the period (`always_invoice`) with the card on file; removing it
credits the unused rest on the next invoice. A workspace needs a plan first.

**One customer's deal**, without a release:

1. In the Stripe Dashboard, create a product for the deal (or reuse an add-on), with a recurring price in the
   subscription's interval (zero for a gift).
2. On the product, **Features → Add feature**: pick one (for example `channels_extra_10`) or create one with
   the metadata above.
3. Open the customer → their subscription → **Update subscription → Add product** → that price, quantity 1.

The workspace's limits change within seconds, when Stripe reports the new features.

## API

| Endpoint | Description |
|---|---|
| <Method m="GET" path="/v1/billing" /> | Billing status: `enabled`, `plan`, `subscription_status`, `plan_expires_at`, `suspended`, `has_customer` |
| <Method m="POST" path="/v1/billing/checkout" /> | Body `{"plan": "plus", "interval": "year"}` (defaults: `pro`, `month`). Answers `{"url": "https://checkout.stripe.com/c/pay/cs_test_a1b2c3"}` to open and pay, or, for a paying workspace, the switched billing page URL |
| <Method m="POST" path="/v1/billing/portal" /> | URL of the Stripe customer portal |
| <Method m="GET" path="/v1/billing/addons" /> | Add-ons on sale (with monthly and yearly prices) and which the workspace has |
| <Method m="POST" path="/v1/billing/addons/{id}" /> | Adds an add-on to the live subscription (`204`); `409` without a plan or when it's already there |
| <Method m="DELETE" path="/v1/billing/addons/{id}" /> | Removes it (`204`) |
| <Method m="POST" path="/v1/billing/webhook" /> | Called by Stripe, verified with `Stripe-Signature` |

The first three need an admin key or a dashboard session; checkout and the portal need the account
owner in an account workspace. When billing is off, checkout and
portal answer `409 not_available`; the portal answers `409 conflict` until the workspace has
started a checkout once. A bad `Stripe-Signature` gets `400`. See the
[API reference](/api/reference) for the full schemas.
