---
title: Manage from your phone
description: Connect your phone to a staqly running on your laptop and check the calendar, move a post or publish one from anywhere on your Wi-Fi.
---

Your agents write posts overnight; you want to glance at tomorrow's calendar over breakfast,
drag one post to the afternoon and publish another right away, from your phone. Connect the
phone once with a QR code; no key to type.

## What you need

- Staqly running on a computer with `staqly serve --expose` or the Docker image, so the
  dashboard is reachable on your network.
- A phone on the same Wi-Fi.

## Set it up

<Steps>
<Step>

### Find your computer's LAN address

`ipconfig getifaddr en0` (macOS) or `hostname -I` (Linux), for example `192.168.1.23`.

</Step>
<Step>

### Open the dashboard to your network

```bash tab="Binary"
PUBLIC_DASHBOARD_URL=http://192.168.1.23:8080 staqly serve --expose
```

```bash tab="Docker"
# add to your docker run command
-e PUBLIC_DASHBOARD_URL=http://192.168.1.23:8080
```

With `--expose` the QR code uses your Wi-Fi address on its own; `PUBLIC_DASHBOARD_URL` pins it
for every browser and makes [alert](/automation/alerts) links open on the phone too.

</Step>
<Step>

### Sign in on your computer

An exposed dashboard asks every browser to sign in once, this computer's included: run
`staqly serve connect-link` (or `docker exec staqly server connect-link`) and open the link.

</Step>
<Step>

### Show the QR code and scan it

**Settings → Devices → Connect a device**, or the command palette (<Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>K</Kbd>).
Scan the QR code with the phone's camera; staqly opens signed in.

</Step>
<Step>

### Use it

The **Schedule** tab shows the calendar as a list by day. Tap a post to open it: **Publish
now**, **Reschedule**, **Edit**. **Posts** writes a new one; **Channels** says when an account
needs reconnecting.

</Step>
</Steps>

## How it works

The QR code carries a one-time link (`/connect#<token>`): it works once, expires after five
minutes and gives the phone its own 30-day session, listed under **Settings → Devices** where you
can revoke it. The API itself stays on `127.0.0.1`: the phone talks to the dashboard, which
proxies `/api/*`. Details: [Phones & tablets](/dashboard/phones-and-tablets).

## Troubleshooting

- **The phone can't open the link**: check that `PUBLIC_DASHBOARD_URL` uses the LAN IP (not
  `localhost`), that the dashboard is exposed, and that no firewall blocks port 8080.
- **"Already used or expired"**: create a new QR code; each link signs in one browser.
- **Alert links open nothing**: set `PUBLIC_DASHBOARD_URL`, which the links are built from.
