title: Limits
description: Every limit a person, an agent or an integration can run into, in one place, with what happens when you reach it.

Staqly has three kinds of limits: what each **platform** accepts, what the workspace's **plan**
allows (cloud only), and what the **API** accepts per request or per minute. This page lists all
of them. When a request goes over one, the [error](/api/errors) says which.

## Platforms

Each channel checks a post against its platform before anything goes out: the text the channel
will actually receive (its own text, else the post's, after [link handling](#links)), its media
and the settings the platform needs. A post over a limit is refused with
`422 validation_failed` keyed `targets.<channel_id>.content` (or `.settings.<key>`);
`GET /v1/posts/{id}/preview` reports the same as warnings without failing.

| Platform | Text | Media | Needs | Links |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Your own automation](/channels/your-own-automation) | no limit | up to 10 images, videos, GIFs or files | — | kept |
| [X](/channels/x) | 280 characters | 4 pictures or 1 video | — | removed (or plain text) |

The other platforms of the [catalog](/channels#the-catalog) are listed as **soon** with their
published limits, so the composer and the preview already check them.

### Links

A channel's **Links in posts** setting decides what its links become when it publishes: kept,
removed, or shown as plain text (`https://example.com/a` becomes `example[.]com/a`). X allows only
the last two and removes links by default. The character count above uses the text after this
change. See [Channels](/channels#managing-channels).

### Platforms on a server

Each platform can be turned off for a whole server with `PROVIDER_<ID>_ENABLED=false` in its
environment, in the Community Edition and in the cloud. A turned-off platform leaves the catalog and the
dashboard, connecting it answers `409 provider_disabled`, and new posts or new targets on its
channels are refused the same way. Posts that already had one of its channels keep it: they can
still be edited and publish as planned. An OAuth platform without its app's keys
(`PROVIDER_<ID>_CLIENT_ID`, `PROVIDER_<ID>_CLIENT_SECRET`) is listed as **soon**. See
[Developer apps](/channels/developer-apps).

## Posts and schedules

| What | Limit |
|---|---|
| Post text | 50,000 characters (each channel then applies its own limit) |
| Files per post | 20 |
| Channels per post, channels per schedule | 50 |
| Tags per post | 20, each at most 40 characters |
| Comments (thread parts) after a post | 25, each up to a day (86,400 s) after the one before |
| Publish time | in the future, at most a year ahead |
| Recurring schedule | times of day (`HH:MM`) with weekdays, or every 5 to 10,080 minutes (a week) |
| A schedule's endpoint call | 60 seconds |
| Waiting for your own automation's result | a collection's processing timeout, 60 s to 24 h, default 1 hour (`PROCESSING_TIMEOUT`) |

## Publishing and other jobs

Publishing, schedule runs and sign-in refreshes are [jobs](/concepts#jobs).

| What | Limit |
|---|---|
| Tries per job | 3, then the job and its target are **failed** (Retry starts again) |
| Wait between tries | about 1, 5 and 15 minutes (up to 20% later, so retries spread out) |
| One run of a job | publishing a target or a comment 10 min, a schedule run 3 min, a sign-in refresh 2 min |
| A job left running by a server that stopped | queued again after 15 minutes |
| Jobs at once | `JOB_CONCURRENCY` (default 4, at most 64); one workspace gets at most half while another waits |
| Finished jobs kept | 7 days (done, failed and cancelled) |

## Webhooks and alerts

| What | Limit |
|---|---|
| Delivery tries | `max_attempts`, 1 to 20, default 8, with growing waits (15 s doubling, at most an hour apart) |
| Timeout per delivery | `timeout_ms`, 1,000 to 60,000, default 10,000 |
| Test sends (webhooks, alerts, channels) | 10 per minute per key or session, burst 5 |
| Alert digest window | 0 to 86,400 seconds |

See [Webhooks](/automation/webhooks) and [Alerts](/automation/alerts).

## Requests

| What | Limit |
|---|---|
| Page size of list endpoints | default 50, at most 200 ([pagination](/api/pagination-and-search)) |
| A listing | 5 seconds, then `503 unavailable`: retry or narrow it with filters |
| Upload | `MAX_UPLOAD_MB` on the server (default 100 MB), or the plan's file size if smaller ([media](/api/media)) |
| `Idempotency-Key` | remembered for 24 hours ([idempotency](/api/idempotency)) |
| Invoices per page (billing details) | 24 |

### Rate limits

| Limiter | Applies to | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Per client IP | sign-in with an API key or recovery key, Google's callback, email codes (start and check), invitations, device links, creating a workspace | 10 per minute, burst 10 |
| Per key or session <Badge>cloud</Badge> | everything else under `/v1` and `/mcp` | 600 per minute, burst 100 |
| Email codes | one per address every 30 seconds, 5 per address per hour, 20 per client IP per hour | fixed |
| Passkey sign-ins | 30 per client IP per 10 minutes | fixed |
| New workspaces <Badge>cloud</Badge> | 5 per client IP per hour | fixed |

Over a limit: `429 rate_limited` (or `code_cooldown` for a code asked too soon) with a
`Retry-After` header. The Community Edition tunes its per-IP limit with `RATE_LIMIT_*`; see
[Monitoring](/self-hosting/monitoring#rate-limits).
