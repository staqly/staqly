title: Analytics
description: See what went out, what failed, where your posts come from and when they do best.

**Analytics** shows how your publishing goes: how many posts went out, how many channels
failed and why, how late the queue was, which agents and automations create your posts, and
when your posts get the most engagement. It is built from your workspace's own data, so it works
the same in [local and cloud mode](/local-vs-cloud). In cloud mode everyone who can read posts
sees it.

Staqly never asks a platform for numbers to draw this page. Views, likes and other platform
numbers are the ones it already has: read when someone opens a published post's **Statistics**
tab, or reported by [your own automation](/concepts#providers). Platforms that don't
report numbers to staqly yet still show everything staqly knows itself.

## Pick a range and filters

Open **Analytics** in the sidebar. One row above the page holds the filters:

- **7 days**, **30 days** (the default) or **90 days**, or a **Custom range** of days.
- **Channel**: one channel's posts only.
- **Source**: posts from one source only (the dashboard, an agent, a schedule).

Days are in the workspace's time zone (**Settings → General → Workspace**), shown under the filters.
Filters are kept in the address, so you can bookmark or share a view.

## What each part shows

| Part | What it shows | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Tiles** | Posts published, success rate, failures, upcoming posts and the average delay | The health of your publishing at a glance. Each tile says whether it is a period total, a period average or right now, and compares with the previous period of the same length (no arrow when that period had nothing to compare with). |
| **Published and failed per day** | Channels published and failed on each day of the range | Spots a bad day or a gap in your schedule. Use the arrow keys on the chart to read each day, or **Show as a table**. |
| **Channels** | Per channel: published, failed, success rate, last post, views, engagements and the most common failure | Finds the channel that needs attention. Click a column header to sort. |
| **Automation** | Per source: posts created and published, channels published and failed, success rate | Compares the dashboard with your agents (over MCP or the API), schedules that fetch posts and repeating posts. A source is who created the post. |
| **Performance** | Views, engagements, likes, replies, reposts and link clicks summed over the range | How your posts do, with how many posts the numbers cover and when they were read. |
| **Posting times** | Channels published per weekday and hour; with numbers, the average engagements per slot | When you post, and when posts do best. A slot with fewer than five posts with numbers is marked low confidence. |
| **Failure reasons** | The most common reasons, how often, on which channels | What to fix first. Each reason links to the failed posts on that channel and to an example post. |
| **Top posts** | The ten posts with the most engagements, or the most recent ones when no numbers exist yet | What worked, with links to each post and to where it was published. |

**Engagements** are likes, replies, reposts, quotes and bookmarks added up, so platforms that
name them differently can be compared. The **delay** is how long after its publish time a
channel's post went out: the queue, retries and the platform's answer included.

## When a number is missing

A channel without numbers says why:

- **This platform doesn't report numbers to staqly yet**: staqly can't read numbers from it.
  Everything else (published, failed, delay) still counts.
- **No numbers read yet**: the platform reports them, but no post in the range has been read.
  Open a published post's **Statistics** tab to read its numbers.
- **No data yet in this range**: nothing was published on that channel in the range.

## For agents and the API

The same report is `GET /v1/analytics` (scope `posts:read`, see the
[API reference](/api/reference/analytics/get-analytics)) and the MCP tool
[`get_analytics`](/mcp/tools#get_analytics). Pass `from` and `to` (days, both included) or
`days`, plus optional `channel`, `collection` and `source`. The SDKs have
`staqly.analytics.get()` and `client.analytics.get()`.

```bash
curl -s "http://localhost:3333/v1/analytics?days=7" -H "Authorization: Bearer $STAQLY_API_KEY"
```
